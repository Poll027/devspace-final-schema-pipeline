mle.prediction_task_definition
tb1 Imagine an operations team wants an estimate before deciding which delivery orders need attention. ‘Predict a late delivery’ sounds clear until two engineers use different decision times or different meanings of late. A task record makes those choices explicit before you prepare examples.
tb2 Define the prediction problem in terms of the inference to be generated and the example data needed to support it. Inputs for a prediction must be information available at its prediction time, not information that only becomes available afterward. An empirical outcome proportion is the count of that outcome divided by the total count in the specified population; a conditional outcome proportion uses only the count in its specified conditioning group.
tb3 Write the task record with one prediction unit, a target event or value, the time a prediction is made and the horizon over which the target is judged, so the example/target relationship is unambiguous.
tb4 For this invented task, one prediction unit is an order at acceptance. The target event is arrival more than 30 minutes after acceptance, judged over that 30-minute horizon. Distance and the promised delivery zone are available at acceptance; the eventual arrival time is not an input. Suppose a supplied population contains 100 orders, of which 20 meet the target event. Its empirical outcome proportion is 20/100 = 0.20. Within a specified zone, 12 of 40 orders meet it, giving a conditional proportion of 12/40 = 0.30. The denominators answer different questions: all orders versus orders in that zone. Record both the population and the horizon beside the counts so another engineer can interpret the same task.

Notice how changing the prediction unit would change the task. If the unit were a whole delivery zone rather than an individual accepted order, neither the input row nor the target event in this fixture would be the same. Similarly, a horizon of one hour would ask a different question from the stated 30 minutes. Keep unit, decision time, target and horizon in separate record fields so those changes cannot masquerade as implementation details. When reporting 0.30 for the zone, say which zone and which eligible orders provided the denominator. The task definition establishes the interpretation that subsequent dataset and model work must preserve.
tb5 A target is not interchangeable with an input. The later arrival can establish whether this example met the target, but cannot become information supplied to a prediction made at acceptance. Nor does the zone proportion describe every zone.
