mle.dataset_selection_bias
tb1 Your collected order sample contains few observations from one delivery zone. Before treating its summary as a picture of the intended prediction population, compare what was included with the supplied target-population counts.
tb2 Data omitted by collection or filtering can exclude observations that matter to the intended prediction population. Compare each category count divided by its population total between the supplied sample and target population to identify supported coverage differences. For a category with known target membership, estimate inclusion probability as included category members divided by all target category members; an empty denominator cannot support a rate.
tb3 Unequal sampling probabilities affect the interpretation of a sample; a descriptive sample summary is not automatically representative of the target population. Compute the arithmetic mean with np.mean(numeric_values) over the selected numeric observations. Compute standard deviation with np.std(numeric_values, ddof=0), which divides squared deviations by N before taking the square root; use ddof=1 only when the stated calculation requires the N−1 divisor.
tb4 Suppose the target population has 60 zone-A orders and 40 zone-B orders, while the sample includes 30 A and 10 B. Target proportions are 0.60 and 0.40; sample proportions are 0.75 and 0.25. With known membership, estimated inclusion probabilities are 30/60 = 0.50 for A and 10/40 = 0.25 for B. These calculations show a supported coverage difference and unequal inclusion. They do not establish that every characteristic of either zone differs. You can also summarize a selected numerical feature with its mean and standard deviation, retaining the specified observations and divisor. If a category has no target members in the supplied denominator, do not manufacture an inclusion rate for it.

Read the ratios as separate conditional questions. The sample's 0.75 zone-A share asks which zone an included observation belongs to. The 0.50 zone-A inclusion probability asks whether a target-population zone-A observation was included. Neither is the target's 0.60 zone-A share. Naming the numerator and denominator in the record prevents a later user from interpreting a selection statistic as population prevalence. The numerical summaries are descriptive evidence for the selected values only. The supported limitation is unequal observed coverage under these supplied counts, not a claim that a particular model must fail or that an unobserved cause of selection has been established.
tb5 A descriptive sample statistic is not automatically a target-population statistic. Keep sample totals, target totals and inclusion denominators distinct instead of treating all three ratios as the same quantity.
mle.label_integrity
tb1 The labels arrive in a different order from the observations. Joining the first label to the first row would give an apparently complete dataset without establishing that each target belongs to the right example. Use the intended observation identity.
tb2 A label must match its prediction or observation through the intended unique identifier, rather than through incidental row order. Check for repeated IDs before building an ID-to-label dictionary, because assigning an existing dictionary key replaces its earlier value. Use set differences between observation IDs and label IDs to detect observations missing labels and labels with no observation.
tb3 Use keyed dictionary lookups to compare each label, observation timestamp and availability timestamp against the task-specific acceptance predicates. Do not treat information unavailable at the relevant prediction boundary as an available input to that prediction. Write assert predicate checks for the declared valid, boundary and failure cases; a false predicate raises AssertionError during ordinary non-optimized execution.
tb4 Let observations have IDs A and B, while the label file lists B then A. A keyed lookup attaches each label to its own observation despite the reversed order. Before constructing the label dictionary, check repeated IDs: two labels for A would otherwise compete for the same dictionary key. Compare observation and label ID sets to reveal a missing label or a label without an observation. Then inspect the matched timestamps under the supplied acceptance predicates. A label's later availability does not turn it into an input available at the earlier prediction time. Exercise reordered, duplicate, missing and declared boundary fixtures so correct row counts do not conceal incorrect correspondence.

Construct the observation-ID and label-ID sets before declaring the join complete. Their differences identify the two unmatched directions: observations without labels and labels without observations. Duplicate IDs need handling before a dictionary silently replaces a value, because set equality alone would not reveal two label rows with the same ID. After the keyed join, inspect the supplied timestamp boundary on the matched record. Key correspondence and time suitability are independent requirements: a correctly keyed label can still be unsuitable under a stated time predicate. This order of checks produces aligned, permitted examples rather than an apparently complete table whose targets belong to another row.
tb5 Matching row counts is not matching identities. A dictionary assignment replaces an existing key's value, so building the dictionary first can hide a duplicate that should have been handled explicitly.
mle.deployment_matched_splits
tb1 The intended evaluation asks how the system behaves on later orders or on groups absent from training. A random division is not automatically the right simulation. Choose and test the boundary supplied for that deployment scenario.
tb2 A random split is appropriate only if its sampling interpretation matches the intended evaluation; dependent grouped or time-ordered data can require different boundaries. Keep observations from a common evaluation group entirely on one side of the boundary, and keep time-ordered training observations earlier than the intended later evaluation observations. Implement the supplied group membership with sets and assert that training and validation group intersections are empty.
tb3 For a time boundary, select training observations before the cutoff and later evaluation observations under the stated scenario, and check the timestamp inequalities. Calculate each partition’s target outcome count divided by its partition total to report the population actually evaluated. Write assert predicate checks for the declared valid, boundary and failure cases; a false predicate raises AssertionError during ordinary non-optimized execution.
tb4 For a group-based fixture, suppose orders A and B belong to customer G1, while C belongs to G2. Keeping G1 wholly in training and G2 wholly in validation satisfies that group boundary; putting A in training and B in validation does not. Assert that the group-set intersection is empty. For a separate time-boundary fixture, use training observations before the cutoff and evaluation observations in the declared later interval, checking the inequalities directly. Report the target proportion within each partition: if validation contains four target events among ten examples, its proportion is 0.40. That number describes the cohort actually evaluated, not automatically the whole intended population. Keep group and time scenarios tied to their respective supplied evaluation interpretation.

The set-intersection assertion asks whether the same group identity appears on both sides, not whether two individual order IDs repeat. Distinct observations from G1 can still violate a group boundary. In the time fixture, compare timestamp extrema or the supplied inequalities under the stated cutoff; distinct group sets do not establish temporal ordering. Reporting each partition's target prevalence helps describe what the evaluation cohort actually contains, but it does not choose the boundary retrospectively. Fix the deployment-matched interpretation first, assign observations accordingly and then inspect whether the resulting partitions satisfy it. The evaluation claim must stay tied to that simulated scenario.
tb5 Random is not a synonym for deployment-matched. Group dependence or time ordering can require a different boundary, and an empty group intersection does not by itself test timestamp ordering.
